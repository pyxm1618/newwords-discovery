# SEO Opportunity Filter

## Core question

For every rising term, ask:

Has this change created a search need that can become a durable SEO asset, tool, product, or long-lived page?

The target is not hot terms. The target is new or renewed search demand whose supply can be built and accumulated over time.

Industry is not a filter. Judge the demand structure, not the category.

## Decision sequence

### 1. Identify the real user job

Determine why people are searching now and what they are trying to accomplish.

Strong opportunity signals are repeatable jobs such as calculate, convert, estimate, check, compare, generate, create, plan, design, customize, find, track, monitor, look up, browse, learn, apply, configure, fix, choose, or use a new product, service, standard, workflow, role, or concept.

A query whose main job is merely what just happened is usually event consumption rather than a durable SEO need.

### 2. Apply the asset test

Ask whether the need can be served repeatedly by something that compounds as an SEO asset.

Possible forms include a tool, calculator, checker, converter, tracker, lookup, database, directory, catalog, structured reference, guide, tutorial, knowledge base, resource hub, template, planner, worksheet, generator, comparison experience, vertical product, workflow, SaaS, community, or interactive content.

Do not keep a term merely because an agent can imagine a product. The user need itself must be supported by evidence.

### 3. Apply the durability test

Ask whether users will continue to encounter this job after the immediate trigger fades.

Evidence can include continued or repeated Trends interest, a new concept or product that persists after launch, recurring user questions, a growing ecosystem of related searches, or a changed workflow, behavior, standard, or market structure.

### 4. Treat events as triggers, not opportunities

The raw event itself is not a KEEP.

Typical raw-event terms that usually become DROP include breaking news, sports results, celebrity incidents, film or TV releases, disasters, short promotions, one-off company incidents, and one-time announcements whose intent is only informational.

Before discarding the whole lead, inspect whether the event has created a new derivative user job.

### 5. Do not invent derivative keywords

A derivative-demand candidate must have real evidence.

Acceptable evidence can include exact query phrasing visible in search results or related-search surfaces, multiple recent pages or questions using the same task phrasing, real autocomplete or keyword-tool evidence, or measurable keyword data.

If the need is plausible but the query wording is not evidenced, keep it as WATCH.

## Opportunity types

### TRUE_NEW

Use when the underlying search need is genuinely new or newly emerging. Earlier history should be absent, sparse, low, or materially different from the recent pattern.

### RESURGENCE

Use when an older term or need has a new reason to matter again and the renewed demand can produce durable search intent. A simple publicity spike is not enough.

### EVENT_DERIVED

Use when an event triggers a new, repeatable user task. The event term itself can remain DROP; the derivative term may be KEEP. The derivative need must be evidence-backed.

## WATCH

Use WATCH when the opportunity may be real but evidence is incomplete. State the exact unresolved question.

## DROP

Use DROP when evidence indicates no durable SEO opportunity, such as pure event consumption, recurring seasonal or match-day demand with no new job, celebrity interest with no derivative task, promotion-only intent, a mature old term merely receiving attention again, or no repeatable user job.

## Trends evidence

Use rolling weekly history as evidence, not as an automatic scoring system. Look for prior-year activity, first-time versus repeated patterns, recent slope, spike and collapse versus persistence, earlier comparable peaks, pullback and rebound, and whether the current level is exceptional.

Preserve null semantics. Do not confuse Trends score with search volume.

## Search-volume evidence

Check Google Ads volume only after a term passes the opportunity filter. Use it to understand scale, not to decide whether a new demand exists. Do not replace SEO competition analysis with Google Ads competition.

## Output reasoning standard

Every KEEP should answer:

1. Why now?
2. What is the user's repeatable job?
3. Why can this become an SEO asset?
4. What does Trends history say about lifecycle?
5. What real evidence supports the query or need?

If one cannot be answered, downgrade to WATCH or DROP.
